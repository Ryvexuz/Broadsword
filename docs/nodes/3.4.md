### Acceptance choices (Lazlo approval required before B2/B3)

The following are proposed engineering budgets, not published accuracy or
measured capabilities. B2 records them as required `destruct34_` settings in
the rigid-body repo's `broadsword.toml`; quoted decimals suit the existing
flat integer/string convention. B3 freezes accepted values and reasons in
`reference/destruction/SUITE.md` before algorithms run. No tuning to hide a
failure. Physical fixture inputs are distinct from acceptance limits.

- A convex rectangular wall uses at least two distinct interior seeds;
  generated cases also cover asymmetric seeds and translated walls.
  Nontrivial chunk count is an exact capability condition, not an accuracy
  tolerance. Arbitrary concave production meshes are outside the first slice.
- Relative volume, mass, centre-of-mass and inertia errors: `1e-9` against
  independent rational box/cell references, normalized by positive fixture
  volume, mass, length and mass-times-length-squared scales respectively.
  Reason: allow f64 clipping/summation roundoff on bounded, well-conditioned
  inputs while detecting lost slivers and wrong inertia frames.
- Handoff relative linear/angular momentum error: `1e-9`, with explicit
  positive mass-times-speed and mass-times-length-times-speed reference
  scales for zero/net-cancelling momentum. Account for prescribed impulses;
  do not compare across a collision as though momentum were unchanged.
- Wall settling: fixed step `"0.008333333333333333"` seconds, at most
  `2400` settling steps, then `240` consecutive observation steps. During
  every observation step, each chunk's speed is at most `"0.01"` metres per
  second, angular speed at most `"0.01"` radians per second, penetration at
  most `"0.001"` metres, and centre drift across the observation interval at
  most `"0.001"` metres. Reason: finite observation and millimetre-scale
  contact/jitter budgets for metre-scale fixtures, not a real-time promise.
  B3 chooses gravity/friction/restitution explicitly. Forced sleep, freezing
  or deleting a moving body cannot satisfy settling.
- Joint failure uses the fixture's separate positive weld/bolt force and
  torque thresholds, equality meaning broken. Below/equal/above cases are
  exact check definitions. Connected components and stable IDs are exact;
  geometric/momentum comparisons use only the budgets above.
- Input bands, maximum bytes/vertices/seeds/joints, work budgets, seed,
  simulation durations, output paths and provenance fields are required
  settings, not embedded limits. B2 must propose and justify finite bands
  before implementation; values remain an open decision, not a claimed
  supported numeric range. Missing/invalid settings fail closed.

B2 must create required keys `destruct34_reference_error`,
`destruct34_momentum_error`, `destruct34_step_seconds`,
`destruct34_settle_steps`, `destruct34_observe_steps`,
`destruct34_max_speed`, `destruct34_max_angular_speed`,
`destruct34_max_penetration` and `destruct34_max_drift` for the budgets above.
It also names each required input/work bound and path key in the contract
and config; no such value may be introduced only as a literal in code.
B9/B10 add the accepted producer SHA, scenario and run identity settings
before their examples use them. This task changes no configuration.

## 5. Test harness

Use ordinary Rust integration tests and std-only deterministic generators;
no external engine supplies the expected answer. Tests print labelled
counts, extrema, errors, settings and fixture identities with `--nocapture`
in diagnostic reruns. Default successful test lines are the d1-d5 markers.
B2 owns one validated settings/input boundary; B6 owns the only body
registration transaction. All library callers and examples use these, not
CLI-only guards.
This sharing is inside rigid-body; the separate crash-sim exporter owns
its local validation implementation of the same versioned file contract,
not an import of B2's Rust code.

| Check | Proof and fixtures to create |
| --- | --- |
| d1 | B2 validation tests; B3 inventory test. Required wall, oblique-cut, sliver, invalid, joined-wall and synthetic internal-frame/car cases with units, explicit expected components and nonempty observation lists. |
| d2 | Exact rational two-cell box, asymmetric multi-seed and oblique-plane cases; independent oriented-edge/face incidence and signed-volume oracle; bounded seeded property cases. Verify coverage by cell intersections, not just a volume sum that could hide overlap. |
| d3 | Known box/inertia references, translated and rotating parent, cancelling momentum, explicit impulse, wrong winding, invalid last chunk and duplicate-event tests at the registration API. |
| d4 | Car frame with a welded panel and bolted subassembly; intact, threshold and repeated events. Independent graph traversal gives expected components; B6's numeric references give momentum. |
| d5 | Seeded wall/impactor/ground scene; full trajectories, contacts and per-body observation extrema; repeat state serialization. Golden pictures alone cannot prove settling. |
| d6 | Real crash-sim scenario and intact control, exporter round-trip, named parser errors, truncated-output and failed-write tests. Artifact includes geometry, node masses/velocities, joint IDs/kinds/thresholds, event times/loads and provenance. |
| d7 | B10 parser/adapter unit tests on B3 data plus the mandatory d6 artifact for the example. Validate actual components against B9's frozen scenario expectation. Never mark the lower-level synthetic case as the integrated check. |
| d8 | B8 deterministic OBJ/manifest sequence, checked frame inventory and human inspection of the same run, not an artist-made animation. |

Fracture computes cells from bisector halfspaces intersected with the wall;
each chunk is closed, capped and has a collision hull. Handoff computes
mass, centre and full inertia in the correct frame. A rotating parent gives
each child its velocity at that child's centre; joint failure aggregates
the actual nodal momentum, including angular momentum about the component
centre. If a deforming component is replaced by a rigid one, report the
projection's kinetic-energy loss: do not claim energy conservation or
increase energy silently. Unsupported internal deformation fails explicitly
under the accepted projection contract. Events use simulation time, not
wall time. Preserve failed-joint history without re-emitting the same body.

Hostile/edge inputs belong in the first harness brief: empty, duplicate and
outside seeds; coplanar cuts, coincident vertices, near-zero-volume slivers;
negative/zero mass; invalid indices and non-manifold topology; signed,
overflowing counts; NaN/inf/subnormal and near-cancelling values; CR and
other unexpected line breaks; unknown/duplicate schema fields; huge input
and exhausted work budgets. Check bounds before allocation, arithmetic or
state mutation; check final finiteness immediately before registration.
Reject unsupported scales with named errors, never clamp or turn them to
zero. Exercise smallest/largest supported inputs and cancellation against
independent exact rational expectations, not this implementation's output.

All artifact paths are configured, relative to the approved root; validate
Windows drive/UNC/backslash forms, traversal, device names and links.
Writer and reader use the same path rule. Test link replacement and a write
failure halfway through: publish only a complete validated artifact using
safe exclusive staging in scratch; preserve earlier evidence and report
the first error. No recursive deletion, shell children or viewer spawning
in this harness. Every example is bounded by work limits and the outer
timeout; they never launch nested Broadsword.

Behavioural negative controls are required, not just compile failures:
omit one cap face (d2 must report open cell); duplicate one child (d3 must
report mass/ID mismatch); disable equality failure (d4 must report component
mismatch); freeze airborne chunks (d5 must report absent contacts); remove
one car event or corrupt its producer SHA (d7 must fail before registration).
Include rejection tests for the validators themselves. Perform mutations
only in task worktrees, restore them and reread the diff before submission.

## 8. Risks and size

- Convex clipping is the first deliverable, not general nonconvex mesh
  fracture. Coplanarity, tiny cells, inertia conditioning and topology may
  require a vetted exact-predicate crate and another Lazlo decision.
- 3.1's API/home and 2.3's mesh contract are drafts. Missing atomic replace,
  convex contact support or deterministic stepping blocks this plan; do
  not implement a toy solver inside destruction to evade the prerequisite.
- Actual car frames may be deformable and store strain energy. Nodal-to-
  rigid projection needs a visible loss budget and calibrated failure laws;
  geometric Voronoi fracture is not material validation or crash safety.
- Crash-sim's joint-failure/public-event readiness is unknown here. B9 may
  need separate upstream capability briefs with newly reviewed claims.
  No attempt is made to assert those APIs exist in this sandbox.
- Settings/path safety, independent reference arithmetic and full inertia
  may exceed a small brief. Re-estimate with source in hand and split
  mechanisms before dispatch; never drop hostile cases to meet a line cap.
- Same-target reproducibility is the initial scope; arbitrary hardware
  determinism and frame-rate targets are not delivered by these checks.
  Viewer approval and every open Lazlo decision in section 4 remain open.
